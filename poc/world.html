The start's kind: a town (the sim's settled area 420 m across the middle) or a village (150 m) set down on a site of the wild land, blending into forests, lakes and the coast beyond; or the game's world on its own, as the game draws it today.

The whole world from one number: ground, water, forests, trails, rocks. Same seed, same world, on every machine. In the STING world it is the sim's seed.

Moves the sun on its track for the day of the year (long days in summer, short in winter). Its colour, the sky, the ambient light and the haze all come from one atmosphere model (single and multiple scattering), so a low sun reddens because the air took the blue. Exposure follows the light.

The season: fresh leaves and flowers in spring, deep green in summer, yellow to red leaves in autumn and bare trees, snow down to the sea and frozen lakes in deep winter. The day's weather is drawn from the season's odds.

The day's weather comes from a hash of the day with the season's odds, blended into the next day's over the night; a storm builds in the afternoon. The buttons force one. Clouds are volumetric blocks that shadow the ground; rain falls and wets the ground; a storm flashes.

Distance to the point you look at, 6 cm to 9 km. Ground detail is refined round the camera; new detail blurs in as it arrives. The lens is a real thin lens, so close views get a macro depth of field.

Cuts mountains into ledges of 5 m: 0 is smooth slopes, 1 is sheer steps. Rebuilds the world. Hills and meadows are not stepped.

With a forced weather: aerosol in the air (Mie density) and a low mist that thins with height above the water. The day's weather sets its own.

With a forced weather: the wind at 10 m in m/s. One wind for everything drawn: trees lean and rock, leaves flutter, grass bends in gusts, waves grow from a measured spectrum, rain slants. The water never lies flat: a residual chop and a swell from weather elsewhere are always there, and the wind adds to them. In the game the sim has its own wind.

The f-number of a 45 mm lens. Lower blurs more in front of and behind the focus. At landscape distances the blur is physically tiny at any setting.
